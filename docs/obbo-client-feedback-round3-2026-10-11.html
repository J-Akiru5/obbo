OBBO iManage · Client Feedback Round 3

Redelivery Sales Inflation, Returns Clawbacks, and Bags vs. Units

Six complaints from the client's third retest (Correctin-2.pdf): redelivery buttons that spin, TOTAL PURCHASED that grows every time a redelivery is dispatched, returns surfaces the client wanted gone, damage returns that claw revenue back out of the books, and a manual delivery-receipt flow that had no way to record a partial bag count. All six were real. The unifying defect was that a redelivery or replacement DR re-recognized revenue the client had already paid for — that is now capped at the purchase order total.

ModelThe one rule that fixed four of the six complaints

A redelivery does not create new demand — the bags were already paid for on the original purchase order. Likewise, a second manual DR against a PO that is already fully recognized books nothing. Every path that writes sales now sums the ledger rows for the whole PO family (the root PO plus every redelivery order that points at it) and clamps this dispatch's share to what is left of the PO total.

1Resolve PO familyroot = linked_po_number for redeliveries, else the PO itself
2Sum booked salesevery shipment_ledger row in the family
3Compute this sharesplit-delivery proration over the PO's sales basis
4Clampremaining = PO total − already recorded; below zero → 0
5Bookbag counts and the delivery log still update; money lines stop at 0
Deliberate exception: admin ledger edits

The cap is intentionally not re-applied in _updateLedgerEntry. Editing a ledger row is a data-correction path, not a new sale — clamping there would prevent an admin from fixing an under-recorded amount. This is documented in the code.

DenominationsUnits in storage, bags on screen

The client's warehouse staff count individual bags, but order quantities and stock balances are denominated in JB/SB units (1 JB = 25 bags, 1 SB = 50 bags). Round 3 makes the split explicit and stops the two from being displayed interchangeably.

Why the columns had to become numeric

A replacement DR for 7 damaged JB bags is 0.28 units. An integer column cannot store that, so the manual-DR form either rejected it or silently rounded. Widening to numeric(14,4) makes the division exact: 0.28 × 25 = 7, verified live. Stock columns stay integer — physical bags are always whole, and every writer rounds bag math at the boundary via wholeBagsFromUnits().

IssuesIssue-by-issue

Real · client issue 1a

Both redelivery buttons spin forever, for JB and SB alike

Fixed

The client ledger checked for a pending redelivery by matching the PO number only. Because a JB order and an SB order can share a PO, a pending JB redelivery also marked the SB order as having one — so both rows rendered a spinner and neither request button worked. The check now matches on PO number and bag type, and the balance summary excludes redelivery rows so they cannot inflate the client's totals.

Before

pending.has(order.po_number)

One SB redelivery spins both the JB and SB orders.

After

pending.has(`${order.po_number}:${item.bag_type}`)

Only the matching bag type shows a pending state.

Real · client issue 1b

TOTAL PURCHASED keeps growing every time a redelivery is dispatched

Fixed

Two independent causes, both now closed. First, the client's Total Purchased summed redelivery orders alongside originals — dispatching a redelivery added its value a second time. Second, dispatchOrder and createDeliveryReceipt recognized the full prorated share with no memory of what had already been booked against the PO, so a second DR re-earned the same revenue.

Fixed by excluding redelivery rows from fetchBalanceSummary and adding the PO-family revenue cap described above to both dispatch paths. A second DR for a fully-recognized PO now books ₱0 sales, gross and net — while the bag counts, stock deduction and delivery log still update normally.

Before

Original DR books ₱222,000.
Redelivery DR books ₱18,500.
TOTAL PURCHASED = ₱240,500.

After

Original DR books ₱222,000.
Redelivery DR books ₱0.
TOTAL PURCHASED = ₱222,000.

Real · client issue 3

Returns were clawing sales, gross and net back out of the books

Fixed

"Delivered (With Returns)" was treating every returned bag as a full revenue reversal. That is correct when a bag comes back good and is restocked for resale — the sale truly did not happen. It is wrong when the bag comes back damaged or wasted: the bag is not restocked, the sale was genuinely made, and clawing the money out overstates the loss and understates revenue.

computeReturnProfitDelta now zeroes total_sales, gross_profit and net_profit for non-restockable returns while preserving the per-bag rates, so the row still reports what the bag originally sold and cost for. Only good (restockable) returns keep the full reversal.

Before

Damage return of 5 JB bags:
total_sales −1,250
gross_profit −1,250
net_profit −1,250

After

Damage return of 5 JB bags:
total_sales 0
gross_profit 0
net_profit 0

The tracking form now forces the distinction

Selecting bags_returned as the tracking status opens a required Category control (Damage / Waste), with copy stating the bags are not restocked and the sales, gross and net figures stay put. The confirm dialog restates the same before committing. The chosen category is written to the ledger row's return_reason instead of a hard-coded value.

Real · client issue 3

Manual DR input had to accept any bag count, including partial bags

Fixed

The manual delivery-receipt form collected whole numbers only, and the schema enforced .int() on JB, SB and quantity. Entering a replacement DR for 7 JB bags — a partial unit — was rejected outright.

The form now converts bags → units at the boundary and stores fractional units (7 / 25 = 0.28). Stock corrections round back to whole bags. The Zod schemas accept any non-negative number on the DR and ledger quantity fields, and the form labels say "JB Bags (individual bags)" with helper text explaining the conversion. Display sites convert units back to bags with wholeBagsFromUnits() so the table never shows 0.28.

2Convert at the boundary7 / 25 = 0.28 units stored
3Stock stays wholeround(0.28 × 25) = 7 bags deducted
4Display converts backtables and reports always show bags
Related · not in the PDF

Warehouse report mixed arrival damage with customer-reported damage

Fixed

Found while implementing the above. The report's closing-stock formula subtracted customer-reported damage as if it were arrival damage, double-counting it against a stock figure that had already been reduced at dispatch. Arrival damage now feeds the closing formula; customer-reported damage is display-only and excluded from the live-stock derivation. The visible "waste" total still shows both.
